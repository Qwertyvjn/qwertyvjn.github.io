01 — Introduction

The planet is warming. Who's counting?

Carbon counting quantifies greenhouse gases — converting every human activity into a CO₂ equivalent number so we can actually measure and manage climate impact.

The rules come from the IPCC (Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change), the scientific body governments use globally. Organizations translate these into the GHG Protocol — the framework most companies actually follow.

You cannot manage what you do not measure.

02 — The Formula

One equation governs everything.

Every emission calculation — from your commute to an entire factory — uses the same core formula.

Activity Data is how much you did: km driven, kWh consumed, kg of fuel burned.

Emission Factor is the CO₂ intensity of that activity — sourced from IPCC databases specific to your region and technology.

CO₂e = Activity Data × Emission Factor
100 kWh × 0.71 kg CO₂/kWh
= 71 kg CO₂e

03 — Scope 1

What's directly yours.

Scope 1 covers emissions from sources you own or control. The most measurable, most accountable category — no one else can claim it.

Examples: fuel burned in company vehicles, boilers, manufacturing process emissions, refrigerant leaks from company-owned systems.

Measured using Tier 1 (national averages) up to Tier 3 (live stack monitoring).

Scope 1 — Direct Emissions In your backyard. You own it, you measure it.
04 — Scope 2

Your energy bill's hidden cost.

Scope 2 covers emissions from purchased electricity, steam, heating, and cooling. You don't burn the fuel — but you bought the output of someone who did.

Example: The CO₂ released at the PLN power plant that generates your office electricity is your Scope 2 emission.

Calculated location-based (grid average) or market-based (supplier-specific, e.g. green energy certificates).

Scope 2 — Purchased Energy Your utility bill. Invisible but real.
05 — Scope 3

Everything else. And it's the biggest.

Scope 3 captures the entire value chain — all indirect emissions upstream and downstream. Usually the largest share by a wide margin.

Examples: business travel, employee commuting, emissions from making the goods you buy, end-of-life treatment of your products.

Hardest to measure. Uses Tier 1 (industry averages) to Tier 3 (supplier-specific primary data).

Scope 3 — Value Chain (Indirect) Your ripple effect on the world.
06 — Tiers

How precise do you need to be?

Each scope can be measured at three levels of data quality — the Tier framework. Higher tier = more accurate, more effort required.

Most organizations start at Tier 1 for most categories and invest in higher tiers only where emissions are biggest.

Tier 1
National / global averagesUse published IPCC or country-level emission factors. Fast, minimal data needed.
Tier 2
Technology-specific factorsRegion or equipment-specific data. Better accuracy, moderate effort.
Tier 3
Primary / supplier-specific dataReal measured data from source. Gold standard — significant effort required.
