01 — The Question

Is heated tobacco actually safer? We ran 100,000 simulations.

Heated Tobacco Products (HTPs) are marketed as reduced-risk alternatives to cigarettes. But marketing isn't data.

This study built a probabilistic health risk model — sampling 100,000 synthetic Indonesian smoker profiles — to compare heavy metal exposure between IQOS (HTP) and a reference conventional cigarette (3R4F).

02 — The Gap

Combustion is the difference.

Four heavy metals were measured in both products: Cadmium (Cd), Arsenic (As), Lead (Pb), and Mercury (Hg).

At combustion temperatures (~800°C), metals volatilize and transfer efficiently into mainstream smoke. HTP heating (~350°C) keeps most metals locked in the tobacco rod.

Cadmium tells the whole story — nearly eliminated.

03 — Non-Cancer Risk

94.77% of cigarette smokers exceed the safety threshold.

The Hazard Index (HI) sums non-carcinogenic risk across all metals. HI > 1 means combined exposure exceeds EPA reference concentrations — a threshold for significant concern.

Cigarette mean HI = 7.02. 94.77% of the simulated population exceeds HI = 1.

HTP mean HI = 0.0797. 0.00% exceed the threshold. A 98.9% reduction.

04 — The Cadmium Problem

One metal. 94% of the risk.

Cadmium's mean HQ = 6.62 out of a total HI of 7.02. Cadmium alone drives 94.3% of non-cancer risk in cigarettes — because it is highly volatile at combustion temperature and transfers efficiently to mainstream smoke.

In HTP, Cadmium drops 99.8%. Arsenic becomes the new dominant metal — but the total risk is still 98.9% lower.

05 — Cancer Risk

70% of cigarette smokers crossed the unacceptable line.

EPA classifies excess cancer risk (ECR) into three zones: negligible (< 10⁻⁶), manageable (10⁻⁶ – 10⁻⁴), and unacceptable (> 10⁻⁴).

Cigarette mean ECR = 1.43 × 10⁻⁴. 100% exceed negligible. 70.19% exceed the unacceptable threshold.

HTP mean ECR = 4.13 × 10⁻⁶. 0.00% exceed unacceptable. A 97.1% reduction.

06 — The Catch

HTP is not zero risk.

While cadmium is nearly eliminated, Arsenic becomes the dominant concern in HTP — driving 76% of non-cancer risk and 94% of remaining cancer risk.

92.41% of HTP users exceed the negligible cancer threshold. But all of them fall within EPA's manageable range. None cross the unacceptable line.

This study covers heavy metal exposure only. HTP aerosol contains other constituents not assessed here. The safest choice remains cessation.

07 — Verdict

Lower risk. Not no risk.

Based on heavy metal exposure, IQOS represents a substantial and quantifiable risk reduction compared to conventional cigarettes for Indonesian adult male smokers.

Cadmium's near-elimination at lower heating temperatures is the primary driver. Arsenic remains the residual concern — within the manageable zone.
